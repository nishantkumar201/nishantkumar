MuseCroc: Embedded EEG Acquisition

Direct acquisition of EEG and motion data from Muse headbands using an ESP32-based module, with local recording and optional wireless communication between devices.

Read the MASc thesis Related conference paper

Why MuseCroc?

Consumer EEG headbands provide accessible sensing hardware, but collecting raw data can involve phone applications and additional software running on a computer. MuseCroc connects to the headband directly over Bluetooth Low Energy, moving acquisition and recording into an external embedded module.

The work investigates a portable acquisition pipeline for experiments involving movement and multiple users. The module communicates with the existing headband; it does not replace its sensing electronics.

How the System Works

Headband connection and sample reconstruction

The ESP32 discovers compatible Muse headbands, establishes a BLE connection, and subscribes to sensor notifications. Incoming packets are decoded into EEG samples and motion measurements. Reconstructing all samples contained in each notification is essential: the headband’s nominal sampling frequency alone does not establish the effective recording rate.

Acquisition and storage

The updated architecture uses ESP-IDF and the NimBLE Bluetooth stack. FreeRTOS tasks separate connection management and acquisition from storage and communication, with buffering between stages to reduce blocking in the acquisition path. Local SD recording provides a way to retain measurements without relying on a continuous connection to a computer.

Optional multi-device communication

ESP-NOW provides a separate link between acquisition nodes and a central receiving ESP32, referred to in the thesis as the Meta-MuseCroc. The documented experiments use a many-to-one arrangement. Timestamps and delivery callbacks support examination of data-transfer timing; this should not be interpreted as a demonstrated guarantee of precise synchronization across every device.

From Proof of Concept to Updated Acquisition

StageWhat changed
Initial proof of conceptDirect headband connection, SD recording and ESP-NOW communication were demonstrated. Incomplete BLE sample reconstruction limited the effective usable EEG rate to approximately 21 Hz.
Updated architectureRefined decoding and buffering restored the full 256 Hz EEG stream reported in the thesis. The firmware moved to ESP-IDF with NimBLE and more explicit task and connection management.

These stages are kept separate because the outdoor recordings were obtained with the earlier implementation. They do not, by themselves, validate the later 256 Hz pipeline.

Experiments and What They Show

Outdoor acquisition with two participants

In the proof-of-concept study, two participants wore independent Muse S–MuseCroc systems during an approximately five-minute outdoor walking trial. Measurements were logged locally and transmitted to a central receiver. The thesis reports stable BLE connections during the trial and successful recording and transmission.

This demonstration supports mobile acquisition feasibility for the tested setup. Its approximately 21 Hz effective recording rate limits the conclusions that can be drawn about higher-frequency EEG content.

Jaw-clenching protocol with the updated system

The updated system was assessed using an approximately ten-second relaxed baseline followed by six jaw-clench events, separated by relaxation intervals of approximately two seconds. Recorded large-amplitude transients corresponded to the clench events and were distinguishable from the resting baseline.

This is a preliminary check of acquisition behavior using a muscle-artifact response visible in the EEG recording. It is not a clinical validation of EEG interpretation or an assessment of diagnostic performance.
